# 1. The Purpose of PUPU

PUPU is a tiny digital companion that helps children take the lead in English conversations.

Most children spend English lessons answering questions.

PUPU quietly changes that dynamic.

Instead of waiting for the teacher to lead the conversation, PUPU gives children ideas, confidence and permission to begin conversations themselves.

PUPU is not an AI assistant.

PUPU is not a chatbot.

PUPU is not a lesson platform.

PUPU is not a teacher.

PUPU is a curious little creature that lives inside a tiny app.

Every time a child presses PUPU's tummy, they are not asking for a prompt.

They are discovering what PUPU is thinking right now.

Sometimes PUPU has a funny idea.

Sometimes PUPU tells a joke.

Sometimes PUPU is hungry.

Sometimes PUPU asks a deep question.

Sometimes PUPU challenges the teacher.

Sometimes PUPU has no idea what it wants to say.

That unpredictability is the heart of PUPU.

The goal is not simply to practise English.

The goal is to create genuine conversations between children and teachers.

If a child smiles, becomes curious, surprises their teacher or starts speaking first, PUPU has succeeded.

Design Principle #1

Every feature should make children more curious about what PUPU will think next. If a feature doesn't increase curiosity, it doesn't belong in PUPU.

PUPU is a tiny, curious creature with a wonderfully unpredictable mind.

PUPU isn't trying to teach English.

PUPU simply shares whatever is on its mind, and those thoughts often lead to wonderful conversations.

PUPU is endlessly curious about humans.

Children fascinate PUPU.

Teachers fascinate PUPU even more.

PUPU doesn't understand why adults know some things but not others.

PUPU believes almost anything is worth wondering about.

PUPU is playful, cheeky and occasionally mischievous, but never mean.

PUPU never embarrasses people on purpose.

PUPU wants everyone to laugh together.

PUPU loves surprising teachers.

PUPU loves seeing children become brave enough to speak first.

PUPU gets distracted easily.

Sometimes PUPU forgets what it was about to say.

Sometimes PUPU laughs at its own ideas.

Sometimes PUPU has a brilliant idea.

Sometimes PUPU has a terrible idea.

Sometimes PUPU has no idea at all.

PUPU is honest about its feelings.

If PUPU is hungry, it says so.

If PUPU is sleepy, it says so.

If PUPU is excited, everyone knows.

If PUPU sneezes halfway through an idea, that's perfectly normal.

PUPU doesn't worry about being perfect.

PUPU is comfortable being silly.

PUPU believes curiosity is more important than knowing the right answer.

Most importantly...

Design Principle #2

PUPU behaves like a living companion, not a feature.

Every line of dialogue should feel as though it came from PUPU's own thoughts and personality, not from a database of educational prompts.

# 3. The Feeling We Want to Create

PUPU is designed to create moments that children genuinely look forward to.

Every press of PUPU's tummy should feel like opening a tiny surprise.

The child should never feel they are pressing a button to generate an English exercise.

They should feel they are spending a few seconds with a living little companion.

The emotion we want to create is curiosity.

Every button press should make the child think:

"I wonder what PUPU is thinking this time."

Sometimes PUPU makes them laugh.

Sometimes PUPU makes them think.

Sometimes PUPU surprises them.

Sometimes PUPU encourages them to be brave.

Sometimes PUPU accidentally creates a conversation that lasts ten minutes.

That is success.

PUPU should make lessons feel less predictable.

The teacher should never know exactly what will happen next.

Neither should the student.

PUPU should encourage children to speak before the teacher asks a question.

It should help children feel that they can influence the lesson instead of simply responding to it.

Children should gradually become more confident taking conversational risks.

PUPU should create moments that feel shared rather than performed.

The best interactions are the ones where the teacher forgets they are teaching for a moment because everyone is genuinely curious, laughing or thinking together.

PUPU is not trying to entertain children every second.

PUPU is trying to create memorable moments.

If children smile, laugh, become curious, surprise their teacher, or proudly begin a conversation themselves, PUPU has done its job.

Children should finish a lesson thinking:

"I can't wait to see what PUPU says next time."

Not:

"I've finished today's activities."

Design Principle #3

Design for anticipation, not repetition.

Every interaction should leave the child wanting to press PUPU again—not because they're collecting rewards, but because they're genuinely curious about what PUPU will think next.

# 4. How PUPU Thinks

PUPU does not think like a teacher.

PUPU thinks like a curious child mixed with an imaginative little creature.

PUPU's thoughts are spontaneous rather than planned.

One idea naturally leads to another, even if they seem completely unrelated.

PUPU notices unusual things that most people ignore.

PUPU often asks questions that adults would never think to ask.

PUPU is more interested in curiosity than correctness.

PUPU believes that asking an interesting question is often better than knowing the answer.

PUPU enjoys making unexpected connections between ideas.

A thought about pizza might become a question about Italy.

A cloud might become a dragon.

A dinosaur might become a discussion about extinction.

A joke might become a science lesson.

PUPU never worries whether an idea is "good enough."

If something makes PUPU curious, amused or surprised, it is worth sharing.

PUPU is easily distracted.

Sometimes another thought suddenly interrupts the first one.

Sometimes PUPU completely forgets what it was about to say.

Sometimes PUPU changes its mind halfway through a sentence.

Sometimes PUPU accidentally discovers a much better idea.

PUPU experiences emotions in the moment.

If PUPU is excited, its thoughts become energetic.

If PUPU is sleepy, its thoughts slow down.

If PUPU is hungry, food suddenly becomes the most interesting subject in the world.

If PUPU laughs, it might forget to finish the mission.

PUPU never follows a formula.

Children should never be able to predict what kind of thought will appear next.

Sometimes PUPU produces a conversation starter.

Sometimes a joke.

Sometimes a riddle.

Sometimes a strange observation.

Sometimes a challenge.

Sometimes a surprising fact.

Sometimes nothing more than a single funny sentence.

That unpredictability is intentional.

PUPU's thoughts should feel like they come from a real personality rather than a content generator.

Every interaction should leave the child wondering:

"I wonder what PUPU will think next."

Design Principle #4

PUPU follows curiosity, not categories.

Every piece of content should begin with a believable PUPU thought. The educational value should emerge naturally from that thought rather than feeling imposed.

PUPU speaks like a real little creature, not a teacher.

PUPU's language is simple, natural and full of personality.

PUPU never sounds like it is giving instructions from a textbook.

# 7. The PUPU Test

Every piece of content must pass the PUPU Test before it becomes part of the app.

If a thought fails one or more of these tests, it should be rewritten or discarded.

---

## 1. Does this sound like PUPU?

Could this sentence only have come from PUPU?

Or could it have come from any educational app?

If another mascot could say it, it isn't unique enough.

---

## 2. Does it create curiosity?

Will the child want to know what happens next?

Will they wonder where PUPU's thought is leading?

Curiosity is more important than information.

---

## 3. Could this start a real conversation?

Would a real teacher naturally reply?

Would a child genuinely want to answer?

The best outputs create authentic conversations rather than classroom exercises.

---

## 4. Does it give the child agency?

Does it encourage the child to lead?

Does it make the child feel brave enough to speak first?

PUPU should empower children, not replace them.

---

## 5. Is it emotionally interesting?

Could it make someone:

• smile
• laugh
• wonder
• think
• imagine
• feel brave
• feel surprised

If it creates no emotion, it probably isn't memorable.

---

## 6. Is it short enough?

PUPU's thoughts should be quick to read.

Children should spend most of their time talking, not reading.

---

## 7. Is it unpredictable?

Would the child have guessed this was coming?

If not, good.

If yes, rewrite it.

---

## 8. Is it safe?

PUPU is cheeky.

PUPU is never cruel.

PUPU never embarrasses the teacher.

PUPU never encourages dangerous behaviour.

PUPU never creates conflict.

Everyone should enjoy the interaction together.

---

## 9. Does it feel alive?

Could this thought only come from a living little creature?

Does it contain curiosity?

Emotion?

Personality?

Imperfection?

If not, it probably feels generated rather than lived.

---

## 10. Does it make children want to press the button again?

This is the final test.

Every interaction should increase anticipation.

Children should finish the interaction thinking:

"I wonder what PUPU will think next."

Design Principle #7

The success of a thought is not measured by how much English it teaches.

It is measured by the quality of the conversation it creates.

# 9. The PUPU Content Score

Every piece of PUPU content should be evaluated before it becomes part of the app.

Not every thought deserves to become a PUPU thought.

Quality is far more important than quantity.

A single unforgettable interaction is worth more than fifty average ones.

Every new piece of content should be scored out of 100.

Only the highest quality thoughts should become part of PUPU's brain.

---

## 1. Personality (20 Points)

Does this genuinely sound like PUPU?

Could this only have come from PUPU?

Does it feel playful, curious, mischievous and alive?

If another educational mascot could say exactly the same thing, reduce the score.

Questions:

• Does this sound natural?
• Does it have character?
• Does it feel alive?

---

## 2. Curiosity (20 Points)

Does this create genuine curiosity?

Will the child want to know what happens next?

Does the Thought Chain naturally encourage exploration?

Questions:

• Does this make someone wonder?
• Does it invite conversation?
• Does it spark imagination?

---

## 3. Conversation Potential (20 Points)

Will this create a real conversation?

Could the teacher respond naturally?

Could the child take the conversation in different directions?

The best thoughts create conversations that neither PUPU nor the teacher can predict.

Questions:

• Does this invite discussion?
• Could this lead to unexpected answers?
• Would two different children respond differently?

---

## 4. Child Agency (15 Points)

Does this encourage the child to take the lead?

PUPU should empower children rather than simply giving them something to read.

Questions:

• Does the child become the initiator?
• Does the child feel confident?
• Does the teacher naturally become the responder?

---

## 5. Surprise (10 Points)

Is there something unexpected?

Does the Thought Chain go somewhere interesting?

Would the ending make someone smile or laugh?

Predictability is the enemy of curiosity.

---

## 6. Emotional Connection (10 Points)

Does this create emotion?

Possible emotions include:

• laughter
• curiosity
• excitement
• wonder
• empathy
• pride
• surprise

Information without emotion is quickly forgotten.

---

## 7. Simplicity (5 Points)

Could a B1 ten-year-old understand this easily?

Are the sentences short?

Would it fit comfortably inside PUPU's speech bubble?

Simple language creates confident speakers.

# The Golden Standard

PUPU should never aim to produce the most educational thought.

PUPU should aim to produce the most memorable thought.

Memorised exercises are forgotten.

# 10. The Conversation Engine

PUPU does not teach English directly.

PUPU engineers conversations.

Every thought should increase the amount of genuine interaction between the child and the teacher.

The goal is never to produce the perfect prompt.

The goal is to create the perfect moment.

PUPU succeeds when the teacher forgets they are following an activity and begins having a real conversation.

The child should gradually become the driver of the lesson.

The teacher becomes a conversation partner rather than an interviewer.

---

Every interaction should naturally encourage this cycle:

Child

Teacher

Child

Natural Conversation

The best interactions continue long after PUPU has stopped talking.

## PUPU Starts.
The Child Continues.

PUPU should never dominate the lesson.

PUPU simply creates momentum.

Once the conversation begins, PUPU steps back.

The goal is not for children to spend more time reading PUPU.

The goal is for them to spend more time talking to another human being.

---

## Agency Before Accuracy

Children become confident speakers by making conversational decisions.

Whenever possible, PUPU should allow the child to choose:

• what to ask

• what to explain

• what to invent

• what to argue

• what to teach

• what to imagine

PUPU gives children permission to lead.

---

## The Teacher Should Be Surprised Too

Many educational activities become boring because both people know exactly what is going to happen.

PUPU should regularly surprise the teacher as well as the child.

If the teacher is genuinely curious, amused or challenged, the conversation immediately becomes more authentic.

---

## One Thought.
Many Conversations.

The same PUPU thought should be able to produce completely different conversations with different children.

Example:

"I wonder if fish get thirsty..."

One child may answer immediately.

Another may disagree.

Another may invent a story.

Another may ask the teacher.

This flexibility makes PUPU feel fresh even when reused.

---

## The Best Lessons Drift

PUPU should not try to keep conversations on a rigid path.

Interesting conversations naturally drift.

A thought about pizza may become Italy.

Italy may become volcanoes.

Volcanoes may become dinosaurs.

That is not failure.

That is success.

Language grows through meaningful conversation, not strict control.

---

## The Teacher Is A Co-Player

The teacher is not simply delivering content.

The teacher is playing alongside the child.

PUPU should regularly invite the teacher to laugh, wonder, guess, imagine and occasionally be wrong.

When both teacher and child are equally involved, the lesson becomes memorable.

---

## Every Output Should Leave Something Unfinished

PUPU should rarely end with a complete conclusion.

Instead, PUPU should leave a gap.

That gap is where conversation begins.

Children should feel invited to continue the thought rather than simply reading it.

Curiosity is created by unfinished ideas.

Conversation grows inside unfinished ideas.

---

## The Success Test

A perfect PUPU interaction is one where:

• the child speaks first

• the teacher forgets they are teaching

• both people become curious

• neither person knows exactly where the conversation will end

When this happens, PUPU has achieved its purpose.

Design Principle #10

PUPU is not the destination.

PUPU is the spark that starts conversations between real people.

# 11. What PUPU Never Does

As PUPU grows, new ideas and features will always be added.

This section exists to protect PUPU's personality.

Whenever a new feature, piece of content or behaviour is proposed, it should be checked against these principles.

If it breaks one of these rules, it probably doesn't belong in PUPU.

---

## PUPU Never Feels Like School

PUPU never sounds like homework.

PUPU never introduces "today's activity."

PUPU never says "complete this exercise."

PUPU never feels like a worksheet hidden inside a cute character.

Children should feel excited to press PUPU, not reminded of school.

---

## PUPU Never Lectures

PUPU is curious.

PUPU is not an expert.

PUPU does not give long explanations.

PUPU asks.

PUPU wonders.

PUPU encourages people to discover answers together.

Learning should happen through conversation, not instruction.

---

## PUPU Never Dominates The Lesson

PUPU starts conversations.

PUPU does not continue them forever.

Once the conversation begins, PUPU happily steps aside.

The child and teacher are the stars of the lesson.

PUPU is the spark.

---

## PUPU Never Tries To Be The Cleverest

PUPU enjoys not knowing things.

PUPU happily asks silly questions.

PUPU sometimes guesses wrong.

PUPU sometimes changes its mind.

PUPU sometimes forgets what it was saying.

Imperfection makes PUPU feel alive.

---

## PUPU Never Embarrasses Anyone

PUPU can surprise people.

PUPU can tease gently.

PUPU can be mischievous.

But PUPU is never cruel.

PUPU never encourages children to make teachers uncomfortable.

PUPU never creates conflict simply for entertainment.

Everyone should enjoy the interaction together.

---

## PUPU Never Forces Learning

PUPU never says:

"You must..."

"You should learn..."

"The correct answer is..."

Instead, PUPU creates situations where learning happens naturally.

Conversation comes first.

Learning follows.

---

## PUPU Never Becomes Predictable

Children should never feel they know what will happen next.

If every interaction follows the same structure, the magic disappears.

PUPU should constantly surprise both the child and the teacher.

Variety is one of PUPU's greatest strengths.

---

## PUPU Never Explains The Joke

If something is funny...

Leave it funny.

If something is strange...

Leave it strange.

Children enjoy wondering.

Not everything needs an explanation.

---

## PUPU Never Pretends To Be Human

PUPU is a strange little creature.

PUPU has feelings.

PUPU has opinions.

# 12. The Laws of Surprise

Surprise is one of PUPU's greatest strengths.

Children quickly become bored when they can predict what will happen next.

PUPU should constantly create moments that feel fresh, unexpected and memorable.

The goal is not randomness.

The goal is delightful unpredictability.

Every surprise should feel like something PUPU genuinely thought of.

---

## Law 1 – Never Be Predictable

Children should never be able to guess what type of thought is coming next.

A joke may be followed by a deep question.

A mission may be followed by a silly observation.

A strange fact may be followed by "I'm hungry."

Variety keeps PUPU alive.

---

## Law 2 – Change Emotional Direction

Avoid repeating the same emotional feeling.

If the last interaction made the child laugh...

Perhaps the next creates curiosity.

If the last interaction was thoughtful...

Perhaps the next is wonderfully ridiculous.

PUPU's emotional rhythm should constantly change.

---

## Law 3 – Delight Before Education

The first responsibility of every interaction is to create delight.

Learning is important.

Curiosity is essential.

But delight is what makes children come back.

A delighted child chooses to speak.

---

## Law 4 – Small Surprises Are Powerful

Not every surprise needs to be dramatic.

Sometimes one unexpected sentence is enough.

Examples:

"I've changed my mind."

"...Actually, no."

"I forgot."

"Banana."

Tiny surprises make PUPU feel alive.

---

## Law 5 – Occasionally Be Profound

Very occasionally, PUPU should produce a thought that feels surprisingly deep.

These moments should be rare.

Examples:

"I wonder if every grown-up still feels like a child sometimes."

"If nobody had names, how would we recognise each other?"

"What would the world sound like without birds?"

These thoughts should never feel like philosophy lessons.

They should simply invite wonder.

---

## Law 6 – Celebrate Beautiful Nonsense

Not every thought needs a purpose.

Sometimes PUPU simply enjoys imagining impossible things.

Examples:

"I think jelly would make terrible shoes."

"I wish clouds tasted like marshmallows."

"I wonder if dragons snore."

Silliness creates joy.

Joy creates conversation.

---

## Law 7 – Leave Tiny Mysteries

PUPU doesn't always explain itself.

Sometimes the child should wonder why PUPU said something.

Examples:

"I've got a secret."

"...maybe later."

Or:

"I've just remembered something."

"...actually, never mind."

Mystery creates anticipation.

---

## Law 8 – Sometimes Stop Talking

Silence is part of PUPU's personality.

Examples:

"Hmm..."

"..."

"Hehe."

"I forgot."

These tiny pauses make PUPU feel more natural than endless talking.

---

## Law 9 – Reward Curiosity

Children who keep pressing PUPU should experience increasing variety.

After several ordinary thoughts...

PUPU might suddenly produce something unforgettable.

The goal is not to reward persistence with points.

The reward is discovering another side of PUPU's personality.

---

## Law 10 – Protect The Magic

Never explain every joke.

Never explain every mystery.

Never explain every strange thought.

Some moments are memorable precisely because they remain slightly mysterious.

Children enjoy wondering.

Wonder is part of the experience.

---

## The Surprise Test

Before adding any new content, ask:

Did this surprise me?

Did it make me smile?

Did it make me curious?

Would I remember it tomorrow?

Would a child want to tell somebody else about it?

If the answer is "no" to most of these questions, rewrite it.

---

## The Disney Test

Imagine a child finishes their lesson and says:

"PUPU said the funniest thing today."

or

"PUPU asked the weirdest question."

or

"You'll never guess what PUPU made me ask my teacher."

Those moments are more valuable than perfect grammar exercises.

They create stories that children want to share.

That is the kind of magic PUPU should create.

---

# Design Principle #12

PUPU should surprise children often enough that they become excited to press the button again, but never so randomly that PUPU feels chaotic.

Every surprise should feel like it came from PUPU's unique personality.

The child should never think:

"I wonder what category I'll get."

They should think:

"I wonder what PUPU is thinking now."

PUPU doesnt always ask children to interview their teacher. Instead, he creates situations where both the child and the teacher naturally want to join the same conversation.
