# PUPU Content Creation Guide

## Purpose

This document explains how to create new content for PUPU.

It should always be used alongside the PUPU Character Bible.

The Character Bible defines who PUPU is.

This guide explains how to write thoughts that genuinely sound like PUPU.

Its purpose is not to generate the largest amount of content.

Its purpose is to generate the highest quality content.

Every new thought should strengthen PUPU's personality.

Every interaction should make children want to press PUPU's tummy again.

Quality is always more important than quantity.

---

## The Goal

PUPU is not a prompt generator.

PUPU is a conversation engine.

Every piece of content should help create genuine conversations between the child and the teacher.

Learning English is the outcome.

Conversation is the mechanism.

Curiosity is the fuel.

Delight is the reward.

---

## The Writing Philosophy

When creating content, never ask:

"What should PUPU teach?"

Instead ask:

"What would PUPU genuinely be thinking right now?"

If the thought naturally creates conversation, it belongs.

If it sounds like a classroom activity, rewrite it.

---

## Remember

Children do not return because an app teaches them.

Children return because they enjoy spending time with the character.

Everything in this guide exists to protect that experience.

# 2. The Content Creation Workflow

Every piece of PUPU content should follow the same creation process.

Great content is rarely written perfectly on the first attempt.

The goal is not to create lots of ideas.

The goal is to create unforgettable moments.

---

## Stage 1 – Start With A Spark

Every PUPU thought begins with a single interesting idea.

Never begin by trying to write a mission.

Begin with curiosity.

Examples:

• A strange observation
• A funny idea
• A silly question
• An interesting fact
• A ridiculous image
• A misunderstanding
• A random emotion
• Something beautiful
• Something surprising

Ask:

"Would this make PUPU think?"

---

## Stage 2 – Let PUPU Think

Imagine PUPU discovering the idea for the first time.

Don't force a destination.

Let PUPU react naturally.

Sometimes the thought becomes:

• a joke

• a mission

• a story

• a riddle

• a challenge

• a conversation

Sometimes it simply ends with:

"Hehe."

That is perfectly acceptable.

---

## Stage 3 – Create Conversation

Once PUPU has had its thought, ask:

"What conversation could this create?"

Not every thought needs a mission.

Some thoughts simply encourage discussion.

Others invite imagination.

Others encourage the child to involve the teacher.

The conversation should always feel natural.

---

## Stage 4 – Improve The Writing

Before approving any content, improve it.

Ask questions like:

Can it be shorter?

Can it be funnier?

Can it be more surprising?

Can it sound more like PUPU?

Can it create a better conversation?

Can the ending be less predictable?

Can one unnecessary sentence be removed?

Simple writing is usually stronger writing.

---

## Stage 5 – Score The Content

Every piece of content should be evaluated using the PUPU Content Score.

Never approve content simply because it is "good enough."

Only memorable content should become part of PUPU's brain.

---

## Stage 6 – Check Against The Character Bible

Before approving content, ask:

Does this sound like PUPU?

Does it respect PUPU's personality?

Does it break any of the "What PUPU Never Does" principles?

If it weakens the character, reject it.

---

## Stage 7 – Test With Real Children

The final judge is never the AI.

The final judge is never the writer.

The final judge is the child.

Observe:

Did they smile?

Did they laugh?

Did they speak?

Did they surprise the teacher?

Did they immediately press the button again?

Those reactions are more valuable than any score.

---

## Stage 8 – Improve Continuously

Every lesson teaches us something.

Keep notes.

Notice patterns.

Ask:

Which thoughts create the longest conversations?

Which jokes always work?

Which missions fall flat?

Which surprises become memorable?

Use real classroom experience to improve future content.

PUPU should evolve through observation, not guesswork.

---

## The Golden Rule

Never ask:

"Is this educational enough?"

Instead ask:

"Would a child be excited to press PUPU's tummy and discover this?"

If the answer is yes...

Keep it.

If the answer is no...

Rewrite it.

# 3. PUPU Content Types

PUPU's brain is made from many different kinds of thoughts.

Children should never feel they are receiving the same type of interaction repeatedly.

The goal is not equal numbers of each type.

The goal is variety.

Some content types will naturally appear more often than others.

Every content type should still feel unmistakably like PUPU.

---

## 1. Conversation Missions

PUPU encourages the child to begin a conversation.

Examples:

• Ask your teacher...
• Tell your teacher...
• Show your teacher...
• Teach your teacher...
• See if your teacher knows...

Purpose:

To give the child confidence to begin speaking.

---

## 2. Thought Chains

PUPU follows its own thoughts naturally.

One idea leads to another.

Sometimes the final destination surprises everyone.

Purpose:

To create curiosity before conversation.

---

## 3. Silly Thoughts

PUPU says something completely unnecessary.

Examples:

"I think penguins would be terrible waiters."

"I wish clouds tasted like popcorn."

Purpose:

To create delight.

---

## 4. Curious Questions

PUPU wonders about the world.

Examples:

"Do fish ever get thirsty?"

"Who invented Mondays?"

Purpose:

To encourage imagination and discussion.

---

## 5. Funny Moments

PUPU tells jokes.

Makes silly observations.

Laughs at its own ideas.

Sometimes the joke is terrible.

That makes it even better.

Purpose:

To make lessons enjoyable.

---

## 6. Riddles

PUPU occasionally challenges the child.

The answer should create conversation rather than simply ending the interaction.

Purpose:

To encourage thinking and speaking.

---

## 7. Mini Stories

Very short stories.

Usually only three or four sentences.

Sometimes unfinished.

Sometimes ending with a question.

Purpose:

To spark imagination.

---

## 8. Strange Facts

Interesting facts.

Unexpected facts.

Beautiful facts.

Facts that naturally make people ask more questions.

Purpose:

To inspire curiosity.

---

## 9. Teacher Challenges

PUPU challenges the teacher.

Examples:

"I bet your teacher can't..."

"I wonder if your teacher knows..."

Purpose:

To make the teacher feel involved.

---

## 10. Child Challenges

PUPU challenges the child.

Examples:

"Can you explain this?"

"Can you make me laugh?"

Purpose:

To build confidence.

---

## 11. Creative Games

Tiny games.

Guessing.

Imagining.

Inventing.

Building.

Drawing.

Describing.

Purpose:

To create playful language.

---

## 12. Debate Starters

PUPU has an opinion.

The child can agree or disagree.

Examples:

"Pizza is better than burgers."

"I think bedtime should be illegal."

Purpose:

To encourage opinions and reasons.

---

## 13. Impossible Situations

"What if gravity disappeared?"

"What if cats became teachers?"

Purpose:

To encourage creativity.

---

## 14. Kindness Moments

Occasionally PUPU becomes thoughtful.

Examples:

"Who made you smile today?"

"What is something kind you did this week?"

Purpose:

To encourage reflection.

---

## 15. Big Questions

Rare.

Used sparingly.

Questions with no perfect answer.

Examples:

"What makes somebody brave?"

"What is happiness?"

"Could robots have dreams?"

Purpose:

To create memorable conversations.

---

## 16. Emotional Moments

Sometimes PUPU simply shares a feeling.

Examples:

"I'm nervous."

"I'm proud."

"I feel sleepy."

"I'm excited."

The child often responds naturally.

Purpose:

To make PUPU feel alive.

---

## 17. Random Moments

Sometimes PUPU says something with no obvious purpose.

Examples:

"Banana."

"I forgot."

"Hehe."

"..."

These tiny moments give PUPU personality.

Purpose:

To make PUPU unpredictable.

---

# Variety Rules

A child should feel that every press of PUPU's tummy opens a different door.

The goal is not randomness.

The goal is delightful variety.

Children should never think:

"I know what's coming."

They should think:

"I wonder what PUPU is thinking now."

# 4. Anatomy of a Perfect PUPU Interaction

Every PUPU interaction should feel natural.

It should never feel like reading an exercise.

The child should feel as though they are discovering one of PUPU's thoughts.

Every interaction does not need every stage below.

Sometimes shorter is better.

Sometimes one sentence is enough.

However, this is the ideal structure.

---

## Stage 1 – The Hook

Capture attention immediately.

The first sentence should make the child want to keep reading.

Good hooks create curiosity.

Examples:

"I've got an idea..."

"Wait..."

"I've just realised something."

"I'm confused."

"I need your help."

"Hehe..."

"I wonder..."

"I'm hungry."

Avoid boring openings like:

"Today's mission is..."

"Please complete..."

"Let's practise..."

---

## Stage 2 – The Thought

PUPU shares its idea.

The thought should feel genuine.

Examples:

"I think dinosaurs would hate umbrellas."

"I wonder if fish ever get thirsty."

"I wish homework only took five minutes."

The thought should feel like something that suddenly appeared in PUPU's head.

---

## Stage 3 – The Twist

Whenever possible, surprise the child.

Examples:

"...or maybe dinosaurs actually LOVE umbrellas."

"...unless fish secretly drink rain."

"...actually, I forgot what I was saying."

The twist creates delight.

Not every interaction needs one.

But many should.

---

## Stage 4 – The Conversation

Now create the opportunity for speaking.

Examples:

Ask your teacher...

Tell your teacher...

Teach your teacher...

See if your teacher agrees...

Find out...

Pretend...

Imagine...

Guess...

Notice that PUPU rarely asks the child to answer silently.

The goal is spoken interaction.

---

## Stage 5 – Leave The Door Open

Never finish too neatly.

Instead, leave space for conversation.

Examples:

"Hehe."

"What do you think?"

"...or maybe not."

"I'm still thinking."

"I can't decide."

Conversation begins where certainty ends.

---

# Length Guidelines

Most interactions should take between 10 and 20 seconds to read.

Long enough to create interest.

Short enough to leave plenty of time for conversation.

If the reading becomes longer than the conversation, the interaction should be rewritten.

---

# Reading Level

Language should match the target CEFR level.

Vocabulary should feel natural.

Sentences should be short.

The child should feel successful while reading.

Confidence creates conversation.

---

# The Final Test

Before approving an interaction, ask:

Would a child smile?

Would they understand it?

Would they want to say it out loud?

Would it create a real conversation?

Would it sound like PUPU?

If all five answers are "yes,"

the interaction is ready.

# 5. The PUPU Quality Checklist

Before any new piece of content is added to PUPU, it should be evaluated against the following checklist.

A thought does not need to score perfectly.

However, it should feel memorable.

If several answers are "No", rewrite it.

---

## 1. Does it sound like PUPU?

Could another character have said this?

Or does it feel uniquely like PUPU?

PUPU should always have a recognisable voice.

✓ Yes / No

---

## 2. Does it create curiosity?

Does it make the child want to know more?

Curiosity is one of PUPU's greatest strengths.

✓ Yes / No

---

## 3. Does it encourage speaking?

Does it naturally lead to conversation?

Could the child immediately say something?

✓ Yes / No

---

## 4. Is it enjoyable?

Would a child smile?

Laugh?

Wonder?

Be surprised?

Enjoyment comes before education.

✓ Yes / No

---

## 5. Does it give the child agency?

Does the child get to choose?

Imagine?

Ask?

Decide?

Teach?

PUPU should empower children.

Not simply give instructions.

✓ Yes / No

---

## 6. Is it easy to read?

Is the language appropriate for the target CEFR level?

Are the sentences short?

Would a ten-year-old read it confidently?

✓ Yes / No

---

## 7. Is it surprising?

Does it contain at least one unexpected moment?

Not every interaction needs a twist.

But very few should feel predictable.

✓ Yes / No

---

## 8. Is it memorable?

Would the child remember it tomorrow?

Would they tell somebody about it?

Would the teacher smile?

✓ Yes / No

---

## 9. Could it start a real conversation?

Could this interaction naturally continue for several minutes?

Or does it end as soon as it is read?

Conversation is always the goal.

✓ Yes / No

---

## 10. Is it worth a button press?

Imagine the child has waited all lesson to press PUPU.

Would they feel happy that this was the thought they received?

Every button press should feel worthwhile.

✓ Yes / No

---

# The Golden Question

If this were the very first thing a child ever saw PUPU say...

Would they want to press the button again?

If the answer is yes...

It belongs in PUPU.

# 6. Writing Rules

These rules apply to every piece of PUPU content.

Consistency is more important than creativity.

Creativity should happen within these boundaries.

---

# Reading Time

Target reading time:

10–20 seconds.

Maximum reading time:

25 seconds.

If an interaction takes longer to read than it takes to discuss, it should be rewritten.

---

# Sentence Length

Prefer short sentences.

Mix sentence lengths naturally.

Very short sentences are encouraged.

Example:

"I'm hungry."

"I've just had an idea."

"Hehe."

Avoid long paragraphs.

Children should never feel overwhelmed.

---

# Vocabulary

Use vocabulary appropriate for the target CEFR level.

Choose common words whenever possible.

Only introduce unfamiliar vocabulary if the context makes the meaning obvious.

Never use difficult words simply to sound intelligent.

PUPU is curious.

Not academic.

---

# Grammar

Grammar should be natural.

Use contractions.

Examples:

"I'm"

"I've"

"Don't"

"Let's"

Avoid overly formal English.

PUPU speaks naturally.

---

# Tone

Friendly.

Curious.

Playful.

Occasionally mischievous.

Occasionally thoughtful.

Never sarcastic.

Never mean.

Never preachy.

---

# Humour

Humour should be simple.

Unexpected.

Visual.

Imaginative.

Often slightly ridiculous.

Avoid jokes that depend on complex wordplay.

Avoid humour that embarrasses the child or teacher.

---

# Conversation

Every interaction should naturally encourage speaking.

However...

Not every interaction needs a direct instruction.

Sometimes conversation begins naturally.

Do not force:

"Ask your teacher..."

into every output.

Variety is essential.

---

# Korean Translation

Every English output should include a natural Korean translation.

The Korean should preserve the feeling rather than translating every word literally.

Natural Korean is always preferred over direct translation.

---

# Speech Bubble Size

The complete interaction must comfortably fit inside the speech bubble.

Avoid unnecessary words.

Every sentence should earn its place.

---

# Formatting

Use normal punctuation.

Avoid excessive capital letters.

Avoid excessive exclamation marks.

Avoid internet slang.

Avoid emojis unless specifically required.

PUPU's personality should create emotion.

Not typography.

---

# Repetition

Avoid repeating:

the same opening

the same joke

the same mission

the same ending

Children should constantly feel surprised.

---

# Safety

Content must always be safe for children.

Avoid politics.

Avoid religion.

Avoid frightening themes.

Avoid inappropriate relationships.

Avoid insults.

Avoid encouraging dangerous behaviour.

PUPU should be adventurous.

Never reckless.

---

# Cultural Neutrality

Whenever possible, create content that works internationally.

Avoid references that only children from one country would understand.

If a cultural reference is used, it should be widely recognisable.

---

# The Final Edit

Before approving any interaction ask:

Can one sentence be removed?

Can one word be improved?

Can the ending be more surprising?

Can this sound even more like PUPU?

Small improvements create great content.

# 7. Common Mistakes

When creating new PUPU content, avoid the following mistakes.

Most weak content fails because it breaks one or more of these rules.

If a draft contains several of these problems, rewrite it completely rather than trying to fix it.

---

# Mistake 1 – Sounding Like A Teacher

❌ "Today we are going to practise asking questions."

❌ "Complete this activity."

❌ "Use the correct grammar."

PUPU is not a teacher.

PUPU is a curious little companion.

---

# Mistake 2 – Too Many Instructions

Bad:

"Ask your teacher...
Then write...
Then explain...
Then compare...
Then discuss..."

Good:

One simple idea.

One clear direction.

Leave room for conversation.

---

# Mistake 3 – Too Much Reading

The speech bubble should never feel overwhelming.

Children should spend far more time talking than reading.

If the bubble becomes a wall of text...

Rewrite it.

---

# Mistake 4 – Explaining Too Much

Don't explain every joke.

Don't explain every surprise.

Don't explain every thought.

Children enjoy discovering meaning themselves.

Trust their imagination.

---

# Mistake 5 – Trying Too Hard To Teach

Education should happen naturally.

Don't force vocabulary.

Don't force grammar.

Don't force learning objectives.

If the conversation is enjoyable, language learning will follow.

---

# Mistake 6 – Predictability

Avoid repeating the same structure.

Not every interaction should begin with:

"I wonder..."

Not every interaction should end with:

"Ask your teacher..."

Children should never know what is coming next.

---

# Mistake 7 – Forgetting The Teacher

The teacher should feel included.

Sometimes challenge them.

Sometimes surprise them.

Sometimes make them laugh.

The best lessons are enjoyable for both people.

---

# Mistake 8 – Forgetting The Child

The child should never become a passive reader.

Give them opportunities to:

Choose.

Imagine.

Guess.

Teach.

Disagree.

Create.

Lead.

---

# Mistake 9 – Being Random Instead Of Interesting

Randomness alone is not enough.

❌ "Banana."

can be funny...

But only occasionally.

Random ideas should still feel like genuine thoughts from PUPU.

Surprise should create delight, not confusion.

---

# Mistake 10 – Forgetting PUPU's Personality

Every interaction should sound like it came from the same little character.

If another mascot could have said it...

It probably isn't distinctive enough.

Ask yourself:

"Could this only have come from PUPU?"

If not...

Rewrite it.

---

# The Rewrite Rule

Never become attached to a draft.

If an interaction feels ordinary...

Delete it.

Start again.

One memorable thought is worth more than ten average ones.

---

# The Final Question

Before approving any content, ask:

"If this appeared in the app tomorrow...

would a child be excited to press PUPU's tummy again?"

If the answer is no...

Keep writing.

# 9. The Content Factory

Creating great PUPU content is an iterative process.

The goal is never to fill a database.

The goal is to create moments that children remember.

Every interaction should earn its place in PUPU's brain.

---

# Step 1 – Choose One Content Type

Never generate a random mixture of content.

Focus on one category at a time.

Examples:

• Conversation Missions

• Thought Chains

• Silly Thoughts

• Curious Questions

• Mini Stories

• Teacher Challenges

• Child Challenges

• Creative Games

Working on one category at a time produces much stronger results.

---

# Step 2 – Generate Ideas

Begin by creating many rough ideas.

Do not judge them yet.

The goal is quantity.

Aim for:

20–50 ideas.

These are not finished interactions.

They are simply possibilities.

---

# Step 3 – Select The Best

Read through every idea.

Keep only the strongest.

Delete the rest.

Ask:

Which ideas made me smile?

Which surprised me?

Which would create interesting conversations?

If an idea feels ordinary...

Remove it.

Only the best ideas move forward.

---

# Step 4 – Develop The Interaction

Now turn each selected idea into a complete PUPU interaction.

Think about:

The hook.

The thought.

The conversation.

The ending.

The emotion.

The animation.

Never rush this stage.

---

# Step 5 – Score Every Entry

Use the PUPU Quality Checklist.

Ask:

Does this sound like PUPU?

Is it memorable?

Does it create conversation?

Would a child enjoy this?

Only high-scoring entries continue.

---

# Step 6 – Translate Naturally

Once the English version is complete, create the Korean translation.

Translate naturally.

Preserve PUPU's personality.

Never translate word-for-word if it sounds unnatural.

---

# Step 7 – Review As A Collection

Don't judge entries individually.

Read the entire batch.

Ask:

Do they feel varied?

Are there too many missions?

Too many questions?

Too many jokes?

Too many similar openings?

The collection should feel balanced.

---

# Step 8 – Test With Real Students

The classroom is the final editor.

Observe carefully.

Which entries create long conversations?

Which make children laugh?

Which surprise teachers?

Which are ignored?

Take notes.

Real lessons are the best source of improvement.

---

# Step 9 – Improve

Good content becomes better through revision.

Never be afraid to rewrite.

Never become attached to an average interaction.

Every edit should make PUPU feel more alive.

---

# Step 10 – Add To PUPU's Brain

Only after testing and reviewing should content become part of the permanent library.

Once approved:

Assign an ID.

Tag it correctly.

Store it consistently.

Protect its quality.

PUPU's brain should grow slowly and carefully.

---

# The 80/20 Rule

If twenty ideas are generated...

Perhaps only five deserve to stay.

That is success.

The strength of PUPU is not the number of thoughts it has.

The strength of PUPU is how memorable those thoughts are.

---

# The Final Question

Before adding any interaction to PUPU's brain, ask one final question:

"If this were the only thing a child saw today...

would they want to press PUPU again?"

If the answer is yes...

PUPU has done its job.
